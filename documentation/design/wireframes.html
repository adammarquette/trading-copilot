Reliability tradeoff: a synthetic order needs the platform live to fire. So protective stops default to native (exchange-held) placement — a disconnect can’t leave a position unprotected. Hidden synthetic stops are an explicit opt-in (safety-critical path, engineering §9).

Stop protection — hidden until it matters, always insured

R-11 · R-5 a synthetic stop that goes native near the level, behind an always-native safety stop
TGT 5248.50
price 5233.0
ENTRY 5230.25
promotion band
STOP 5222.00synthetic → native
SAFETY 5216.00always native

Far from the stop

The actual stop is synthetic / hidden — nothing rests on the book, so there's nothing to hunt. (Price is currently above entry.)

Within the band

Price comes within a configurable proximity of the stop (ticks / ATR / % of the entry→stop distance) → the actual stop is promoted to a native working order, now exchange-held for the moment it might fill.

Safety stop — always native

A catastrophic stop rests beyond the actual stop, always at the broker, placed at your configurable max drawdown per trade. It catches gaps, fast moves, and platform/connection outages — the position is never without a real stop, and its loss stays inside your account limits (R-5).

Suggestion lifecycle — when the setup doesn’t come

R-4 · R-9 price drifts away → scratched, not chased

Active

Valid 0:48 · price within tolerance of the entry — takeable now.

→

Stale

Price drifted past the entry tolerance — the take is blocked at the gate (R-12); shown greyed, one tap to re-request.

→

Expired / void

Validity window elapsed, or the thesis broke — scratched as a no-trade.

Pending · on-trigger
Waiting for 5230.25 — cancels if price drifts a set amount against it, or the window / session ends. It never fires on a stale setup.
A scratch resolves as no-fill — not a loss (R-9), and it feeds the trigger-rate metric. If the setup re-forms, the engine issues a new superseding suggestion rather than chasing price.

The native safety stop still holds. Synthetic orders live in the app, so a dropped link orphans them — but the always-native safety stop rests at the exchange and caps the position regardless. The AuditRecord flags synthetic_risk so the exposure stays queryable after the fact.
Auto-flatten is safety-critical (R-13): it runs from a deterministic timer, prefers native (exchange-held) actions so a connection blip can't strand a position, verifies flat, and writes an immutable audit record (eng §9). The manual kill switch is the same sequence on demand — always visible at every breakpoint.
