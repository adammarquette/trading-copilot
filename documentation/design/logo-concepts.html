Logo / app-icon concepts

Four directions for the Trading Co-Pilot brand mark and PWA app icon. Drawn in the ADR-0005 tokens — cool near-black ground, the reserved accent blue — for the installable client in ADR-0010 (R-19). Mid-fidelity: shape and story, not final vector polish.

The governing constraint. ADR-0005 keeps semantic trading colors separate from the accent — green/red/amber mean long, short, and time-to-flat, and nothing else. So the mark is built in the accent blue #9DC3FF, not the green up-and-to-the-right every other trading app uses. That's a constraint worth leaning into: it makes the icon read as an instrument, not a bull-market sticker, and it means the icon never competes with live position status.

Concept A — Co-Pilot Chevron

Safest · most legible

Two chevrons, one leading

Aviation rank insignia read as a pair — pilot and co-pilot — and a chevron is also the cleanest abstraction of a move in your favour. The lead chevron is solid, the second dimmed: the AI proposes, the human commands, which is the actual product thesis (R-4 suggest → R-11 approve). No literal candlestick, so it stays calm at any size.

Strength: survives 16 px intact — the only concept here that truly does.  ·  Risk: double-chevron is common shorthand; distinctiveness comes from palette, not silhouette.

Concept B — Candle Sequence

Most literal

Three candles, gaining conviction

An ascending three-bar sequence rendered in the accent, with opacity climbing left to right — the past is faint, the current bar is bright. Instantly says "futures trading" with zero explanation, and the opacity ramp carries a second meaning: evidence accumulating into a decision.

Strength: unambiguous category signal; the chart is the product (R-10).  ·  Risk: three bodies plus wicks is a lot of detail — the gaps close up below ~24 px. Would need a simplified two-candle variant for favicon use.

Concept C — Governor Arc

Most on-thesis

Upside, bounded by a gauge

An open dial — the daily risk governor and the AI-spend meter both already live in the wireframes as gauges — with a chevron rising through the gap at its top. The story is the whole product in one shape: upside contained by a limit. The arc is the risk gate (R-5), the chevron is the trade, and the gap is the only way through. It also reads as a "C".

Strength: the only mark here that encodes the safety thesis rather than just the category.  ·  Risk: the thin arc softens at small sizes; needs a heavier-stroke small variant.

Concept D — HUD Reticle

Most distinctive

A cockpit sight, framing the setup

ADR-0005 calls the UI a "single-operator, information-dense, safety-critical cockpit" — this takes that literally. Four corner brackets frame a chevron: a heads-up targeting reticle locked onto a setup. Leans hardest into the co-pilot/aviation half of the name and looks least like a fintech template.

Strength: nothing else in the category looks like this; the frame is reusable as a UI motif (focus states, the suggestion card).  ·  Risk: highest detail count — the brackets go to mush at 16 px and need a bracket-free small variant.

Concept E — Candle Reticle (a cockpit locked on a setup)

Hybrid · B + D · operator's lean

The setup, framed by the sight

The candle sequence (B — the setup, evidence accumulating) sits inside the HUD reticle (D — the cockpit sight locked onto it). "A cockpit locked on a setup": category recognition and the co-pilot / aviation half of the name in one mark, still accent-blue-only. The reticle frame is reusable as a UI focus motif (suggestion card, focus states).

Strength: says futures and co-pilot at once; distinctive without a literal bull-market cliché.  ·  Risk: highest detail here — both the brackets and the candle gaps soften below ~24 px; wants a simplified small variant (drop to two candles / heavier brackets).

Recommendation: C (Governor Arc), with A as the fallback. (Update — the operator likes the B + D hybrid, E · Candle Reticle ("a cockpit locked on a setup"); it's now wired as the provisional app-icon.svg. Still open — its small-size variant is the same fixable weakness as C's.) C is the only one whose shape argues the product's actual thesis — bounded upside, a limit you go through rather than around — and it reuses the gauge language already in the wireframes. Its one weakness (small-size softening) is fixable with a heavier-stroke ≤24 px variant, which is standard practice. Pick A instead if you'd rather not maintain two optical weights.
